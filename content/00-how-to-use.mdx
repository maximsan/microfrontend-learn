### Reading paths

The modules build on each other, but they are written to survive being read out of order.

- **The full path (<Mod to="how-to-use" num />–<Mod to="migration" num />).** Start here if you want the mental model rebuilt from the foundation. Part I is conceptual and historical; Parts II and III are operational; Part IV turns it into decisions and migration plans.
- **The decision path (<Mods to={["dividing-line", "spectrum", "rendering-modes", "choosing", "synthesis"]} />).** You have a choice to make this quarter and need the axis, the options and the trade-offs. About half an hour.
- **The micro-frontend path (<Mods to={["navigation", "micro-frontends", "sharing", "streaming", "federation", "auth", "sessions", "delivery", "migration"]} />).** The decision to split the frontend is already made and you need routing, composition, styling and state, bundling, auth and delivery. This is the densest material here.
- **The hands-on path.** Read a module, then do its lab: the brief at the top of each module names it. The labs live in `labs/` in the repository and reproduce the chapter's traps on your own machine, most with tests. <Mod to="capstone" /> puts the main defaults together in one small working system, with acceptance tests you can also run against your own rebuild.

### The evidence rule

This is a fast-moving area where a great deal of confidently-written advice is two to five years stale, and where popular tools have been quietly archived or deprecated. So the rule is simple: **any claim a reasonable engineer might dispute carries a link to a primary source** — a spec, an RFC, official documentation, or the maintainers' own words. Secondary sources are labelled as such.

Where a fact could not be confirmed to that standard it is marked inline and collected in <Mod to="caveats" />. That appendix also records widely repeated claims that are *wrong* — including some earlier versions of this guide made — because knowing which way the ground moved is often more useful than the corrected fact.

Boxed text marked **From the source** is verbatim from a spec, RFC or official documentation, with a link. Everything else — boxed or not — is my analysis.

### Reading the callouts

- **Insight** — the author's analysis: a point worth carrying out of the chapter.
- **Trap** — a mistake that is easy to make and expensive to find, with the fix.
- **From the source** — a verbatim quote from a spec, RFC or official documentation, ending with its link.
- **Ground has moved** — something that changed recently, where older articles and tutorials are now wrong.
- <Rev kind="oneway" /> <Rev kind="costly" /> <Rev kind="cheap" /> — how expensive a decision or migration step is to undo (<Mod to="synthesis" />).

Every module opens with a brief — time, prerequisites and what you will be able to do — and closes with a **Recap** and a **Check yourself**. Try the questions before opening the answers; they are written to be answered from the chapter, not from memory of other sources.
